Prepared 2026-10-06 for counsel (plan item C17). Branch `feat/sta-377-measurement-privacy`, showcase.
It builds on the STA-373 pre-review (`docs/features/STA-373/legal-review.md`). It is not legal advice.

**This is a draft. It must not reach `main` before counsel signs off.**

## 1. Scope

**Changed** in all four policies: §5.1, §5.2, §5.3, §5.5, §5.6, the US paragraph of §6, and two rows of §8.
English is the source. French, Spanish and Polish follow it and are written natively (fr: "vous"; es: "tú"; pl: "ty", no gendered address, "Twój" capitalised as in the existing §5.5).

- the consent version (still 3);
- the "Last updated" date (still 3 October 2026; see G4).

## 3. Gating items

**G1. `cookies.prefs.us.analytics.body`: "Google handles this data on our behalf only." This is true only once the GA4 admin settings of plan C9 are applied:** Google signals off, ads personalization off (all regions), no ads data sharing, user-provided data collection off, retention as counsel sets. The code-side flags (`allow_google_signals:false`, `allow_ad_personalization_signals:false`) do not make it true on their own, and no Google Ads link may exist (C10). Until C9 is done and checked, the sentence must not ship.

**G2. Flipping D4.** The matrix flag `analytics_under_us_opt_out` ships as `off`: under a US GPC signal both advertising and audience measurement are off, and the policy (§5.1 ¶3) says so. If counsel confirms the service-provider basis (L2) and C9 is applied, the flag flips to `service_provider`, and these change together: §5.1 ¶3 ("audience measurement is also turned off"), `cookies.prefs.us.gpc.statusAll`, the US Analytics lock, runbook RG-04 and RG-07.

**G3. The time-zone statement is interim.** "We do not use your IP address for this" (§5.1 ¶5) holds until STA-379. At that change the policy must disclose the IP-to-country step and its processor (L7), and list the new strictly necessary region cookie (`stampeo_region`, 1 day) in §5.2. It is not listed now because nothing writes it in STA-377. This draft does not name the later mechanism.

**G4. Date, notice and consent version.**
- "Last updated" is left at 3 October 2026. Counsel (or Harry) sets it at sign-off.
- §13 promises an email to Business users on substantial changes (STA-373 L9 was the same question).
- The consent version stays 3, as instructed. The text now describes processing the STA-373 text did not: the campaign source is kept for a visitor who allowed only one of the two purposes (J3), Google receives a session identifier (F5), and the first step is reported at account confirmation, before any business. **Question:** does that require re-asking EU visitors (a consent version bump), or does the existing consent cover it?

## 5. Wording the facts did not settle, and gaps found

**J1. `stampeo_consent` row.** The brief's purpose is "your choice". The row keeps "and the random identifier that links your successive choices" because the cookie still carries the subject id (contract C-2, field `s`), §5.6 says so, and a test pins that the row mentions the identifier and points to 5.6.

**J2. "Generated on your device" dropped.** The identifier is minted in the browser, or by our cookie route when none exists. §5.6 now says only that it is random and not derived from anything about you.

**J3. `stampeo_src` category "Audience measurement or advertising".** The old text kept the campaign source only for a visitor who accepted cookies and "reached this site from an advertisement". The new text keeps it while either purpose is allowed, whether or not the visit came from an ad. §5.5 ¶1 says each cookie is kept only while the category listed for it in 5.3 is allowed.

**J4. Legacy cookie sentence.** The brief says "older browsers may still hold it until it expires". The draft says "a browser that received it earlier may still hold it until it expires", because "older browsers" can be read as outdated browsers.

**J5. Identifier sentence in §5.5 ¶3.** Old: "a Google click is never reported to Meta, and a Meta click is never reported to Google". New: "a Google click identifier is never sent to Meta, and a Meta click identifier is never sent to Google". Reason: GA4 now also receives a `sign_up` for a Meta-click sign-up (plan AC10.3, with campaign parameters), so the absolute claim about clicks would overstate. The identifier claim stays true. Not one of the nine facts.

**J6. §6 recipients.** "Meta and the other advertising platforms named in 5.3" became "Meta and Google (5.5)". TikTok is gone, and Google is the only other platform.

**J7. US explicit grants.** The matrix gives every row a 182-day grant. The `stampeo_consent` row reads "6 months. In the United States, a refusal is kept 13 months and renewed on each visit", which implies 6 months for a US grant. The facts do not state it separately.

**J8. "When the signals disagree".** Kept from the brief. Until the later change there is one signal, the time zone, so the sentence is forward-looking. Counsel may prefer to drop it until then.

**J9. "We do not use your IP address for this".** The brief says "today". The policy sentence is present tense without the word. §5.4 separately says PostHog receives the visitor's IP for logging: the new sentence is about choosing the rules only.

**J10. Regions.** "6 months, then we may ask again" sits in the paragraph about the opt-in flow (§5.1 ¶2). The regions are not listed one by one.

**J11. "If you create an account" (the brief's wording) may read as covering employees or invited users who create an account.** Nothing is reported for them: only the owner sign-up on the marketing site triggers the first step. Alternatives: "If you create an account on this site", or "If you sign up as a business owner".

**J12. Hashed details at the first step.** The Meta bullet still lists the business's country, city and postcode. At account confirmation no business exists (plan L11: a subset), so the list over-discloses for that step. Left as is.

**J13. Per-locale choices.**
- fr: "commerce" for the business in 5.5 (as the bullet already did); "régie publicitaire" kept.
- es: "uso compartido" in §5.1 to match the UI title "Venta, uso compartido y publicidad dirigida", while §6 keeps «compartir»; the policy says "rechazo" where the UI says "negativa".
- pl: "zasady ścisłe (opt-in)" for the strict rules; "Twój/Twoje" capitalised as in §5.5; the policy says "pomiar ruchu" where the UI says "Statystyki" for audience measurement (as before this change).
- The labels of the US control in fr/es/pl are the UI strings of `footer.privacyChoices`, copied word for word (pinned by test).

**J14. Deletion of attribution data.** §5.5's last sentence and the §8 row "Advertising attribution (click identifier, campaign): deleted with the Business account it belongs to" are unchanged. Since D14 the person's attribution is first stored against the user account (`account_ad_attribution`, deleted with the user), so someone who never creates a business has no stated deletion rule. The facts are silent. Proposed: "Deleted with the account it belongs to (the Business account, or the user account if no business was created)".

**J15. §5.6 record contents.** The text says each record holds the decision "and nothing about you": categories, text version, opt-in or opt-out, surface, two timestamps. The ledger now also stores the policy-matrix version, the rule set that applied (EEA/UK/CH, US or other, derived from the time zone) and whether GPC was on (migration 189, `lib/consent-ledger.ts`). Not drafted, because the facts do not list it. Proposed sentence: "...and which set of rules applied to you (see 5.1) and whether a Global Privacy Control signal was on."

**J16. 13 months and 400 days.** The US refusal cookie lives 400 days; the policy says 13 months (about 395 days), so the written period is slightly shorter than the real one.

**J17. GA4 retention.** The policy does not state how long Google keeps GA4 data. Plan C9 leaves it to counsel; §8 has no row.
