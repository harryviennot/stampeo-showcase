description: What every number and chart on the Analytics page means, how a visit is counted, how much history each plan shows, and why a section may say it unlocks later.
---

The **Analytics** page shows how your loyalty program is doing over time and when your customers come in. This page explains each number in plain words, so you know what you are looking at before you act on it.

> Owners and admins can open **Analytics**. People who only scan with the app do not see it.

## Open the page

Click **Analytics** in the sidebar, just below **Activity**. You can also click **See trends** at the top of **Activity**.

The page is built from a few sections, always in this order: the period and the numbers at the top, **Visits over time**, **Busiest times**, **How often they come**, **Reward progress**, and on the Pro plan **Monthly cohorts**.

## The period, and when it ends

Pick a period with the buttons under the title: **7 days**, **30 days**, **90 days** or **1 year**. Which ones you can pick depends on your plan (see "How much history your plan shows" below). On the device you use, the page remembers the last one you picked for each business.

A period is a number of whole days that **ends yesterday**, counted in your business time zone. Today stays on **Activity**, so a number does not drop every morning just because the day is not over yet. The line under the buttons states the exact dates and the time zone.

Every figure is compared with the same number of days just before. A small arrow shows the change only when there is something to compare with. A business that is only a few weeks old shows no arrow, rather than an invented percentage.

## What counts as a visit

A **visit** is one customer at your counter.

A visit is counted when your team:

- scans a customer's card in the scanner app and adds stamps or points, or
- redeems a reward for a customer.

If the same customer is recorded again less than an hour after their previous scan, it is still the same visit. Redeeming a reward and then adding a stamp is one visit. A scan in the morning and another in the afternoon are two.

These are not visits:

- Stamps or points you add by hand from the dashboard (marked **Manual** on **Activity**). They still count on the customer's card. The time on them is when you typed them, not when the customer came in, so they would distort every chart about timing.
- Scans you voided.
- A card converted between stamps and points.

The customer panel on **Customers** counts **Total Visits** the same way.

## The numbers at the top

**Visits** is the number of visits in the period. Under it, "from 40 customers" tells you how many different customers made them.

**New customers** is the number of people who signed up in the period. It is the same idea as **This month** on **Customers**, but over whole days ending yesterday, so the two can differ slightly.

**30-day return rate** is the share of first-time customers who came back on another day within 30 days of their first visit. To give every customer a full 30 days to come back, it looks at customers who had their first visit in the period moved 30 days earlier. For the last 30 days, that means first visits from 60 to 30 days ago. A second visit on the same day does not count as coming back. It needs 10 first-time customers: until then it shows a dash and says how many it is waiting for.

**Tracked spend** appears on points programs only. It adds up the ticket amounts your team entered at the counter, and leaves out visits without an amount. The line under it gives the average ticket.

## Visits over time

Each bar is a day (for 7 and 30 days), a week (for 90 days) or a month (for 1 year). A bar has two parts: **Returning** for customers who had already visited before, and **First visit** for customers coming for the first time. The subtitle tells you how much of the total comes from returning customers.

A dot under a bar means you sent a broadcast that day, so you can see whether a spike lines up with one.

Click or tap a bar to see its numbers in the line under the chart. Until you pick a bar, that line sums up the whole period. To go back to the whole period, click or tap the same bar again, click anywhere outside the chart, or press Escape.

## Busiest times

A grid of the days of the week and the hours of the day. The darker a cell, the more visits. Each cell stands for the **average number of visits in a week** at that day and hour, so "6.5 visits a week" means about six or seven visits at that hour on a typical week of the period.

The grid starts on Monday (on Sunday for businesses in the United States). It shows the hours from your first to your last visit of the day, including a business that stays open past midnight, so a lunch break shows up as a gap of empty rows.

Two lines sit above the grid:

- **Busiest** is the day and hour with the most visits in a typical week.
- **Quietest open hour** is the quietest hour you are open. Only hours with visits in at least half of the weeks of the period count, so the hours you are closed are not mistaken for quiet ones.

Click or tap a cell, or one of the two lines, to see its day, its hour and its weekly average. Click it again to let it go.

It needs 50 visits in the period before it draws, because a handful of visits would make one lucky hour look like a pattern.

## How often they come

This groups the customers who visited in the period by the usual gap between their visits:

- **About every week**: usually 10 days or less between visits.
- **Every 2 weeks**: up to 21 days.
- **About once a month**: up to 45 days.
- **Less often**: more than 45 days.
- **Came once**: only one day with a visit so far, so there is no gap to measure yet.

Each customer is counted once, in the group that fits them best. Two visits on the same day count as one day, because the gap is measured in days. The groups are counted when you open the page, from the visits up to the last day of the period you picked, so an earlier period shows how things stood then. You see totals only, never which customer is in which group, and nothing is predicted. The subtitle gives the share of customers who come at least every 2 weeks.

It needs 20 customers with two visits or more.

## Reward progress

This section shows where your customers are on their card **today**. It is a snapshot, so it does not change with the period.

On a stamps program, there is one column for each number of stamps on the card, and a star column for customers who hold a reward. For cards with many stamps, columns group stamps two by two. On a points program, customers are grouped by points balance, in bands between your reward thresholds.

Each column is split in two:

- **Still coming**: customers who visited in the last 30 days.
- **Not back for 30+ days**: customers who have not.

This shows you where customers give up on your card. Many customers who have not come back, halfway through the card, can mean the reward feels too far away.

The line under the chart counts the rewards redeemed in the period you picked. That one number follows the period. Owners also see **Change the reward** (or **Change the rewards**), which opens the program settings.

It needs 20 customers with a card.

## Monthly cohorts

Available on the Pro plan.

A cohort is the group of customers whose first visit was in the same month. For each month, the table shows how many of them came back on another day within 30, 60, 90 and 180 days. It shows up to 12 months, newest first.

- A dash means that window has not passed yet for the whole month.
- A month with fewer than 10 customers is faded and shows no percentage, because a rate on a few people means little.
- Click or tap a cell to read it as a sentence, for example "Of 42 customers whose first visit was in March, 15 (36%) came back within 60 days."

The 30-day column follows the same rule as the **30-day return rate**, so the two always agree. Cohorts always count your whole business, even when a location is picked.

It needs 2 complete months of history and 10 first-time customers across them. On Starter and Growth, a blurred preview of this table appears once your return rate has enough customers behind it.

## How much history your plan shows

The period buttons you can pick depend on your plan:

| Plan | Periods |
|---|---|
| Starter | 7 days, 30 days |
| Growth | 7 days, 30 days, 90 days |
| Pro | 7 days, 30 days, 90 days, 1 year, plus monthly cohorts and the location filter |

A period your plan does not include has a crown on its button. Clicking it opens a panel that names the plan that unlocks it. Nothing changes on the page.

If your business is younger than the period, the panel says so: there is nothing older to show yet, and the history fills in as your business grows.

Your data is never deleted when you change plan. If you move to a smaller plan, the page simply shows less. If you move up again, the full history is there straight away. A feature Stampeo adds to your plan for a limited time works the same way until it ends.

## Filter by location

Available on the Pro plan, for businesses with two or more locations.

A **Location** filter appears under the period buttons. Picking a location changes **Visits**, **New customers**, the return rate, **Visits over time**, **Busiest times** and **How often they come**.

**Reward progress** and **Monthly cohorts** always cover your whole business. They carry an **All locations** tag while a location is picked, so you know.

For a location, the return rate looks at customers whose first visit was at that location, and counts them as back if they visit again anywhere. Visits recorded before you had locations only appear when no location is picked, unless you linked your past activity to your first location when you added it.

To open **Analytics** with a location already picked, click **See trends** in that location's panel. For a location's scans, sign-ups and rewards, see [How locations work](/help/how-locations-work).

## Why a section says it unlocks later

A section needs enough customers or visits to say something true. With 3 customers, a return rate of 100% or 0% tells you nothing. So instead of an empty or misleading chart, a section waits, and appears by itself once there is enough.

Sections that are waiting are listed together in one card, **Unlocks as you grow**. Each row shows the section, how far along it is, and what it is counting, for example "27 of 50 visits in the last 7 days". The numbers it waits for are:

| Section | What it needs |
|---|---|
| Busiest times | 50 visits in the period |
| 30-day return rate | 10 first-time customers with 30 days behind them |
| How often they come | 20 customers with 2 visits or more |
| Reward progress | 20 customers with a card |
| Monthly cohorts | 2 complete months of history and 10 first-time customers across them |

**Visits over time** draws from your very first visit, because a few bars are an honest picture. If your business has no visit at all yet, the page shows **No visits yet** with a **Share your card** button instead of charts.

A longer period often unlocks a section: 50 visits are easier to reach over 90 days than over 7.

## When your day starts

Every day and every hour on this page is counted in your business time zone. If the busiest hour looks a few hours off, your time zone is probably not the one you expect. Check it in **Settings**: see [Set your business time zone](/docs/account-and-settings/set-your-time-zone).

The first time a business opens **Analytics**, the page says **We are preparing your analytics** while it builds your history. This takes a few minutes, and the page updates by itself. The same happens after you change your time zone, with the message **Recalculating your analytics in your new time zone**.

## Troubleshooting

**The visit I just made is not on the page**

A period ends yesterday, so today's visits are on **Activity**, not here. For earlier days, the figures refresh about every ten minutes.

**A section is missing, or the page is shorter than I expected**

A section that does not have enough data yet moves into the **Unlocks as you grow** card. Open it to see how close it is. Picking a longer period can help.

**The numbers do not match Activity or the home page**

**Activity** and the home page count every scan, including stamps added by hand, and their days follow a different clock, so they can differ from visits here, mostly around midnight. **Analytics** counts visits, in your business time zone.

**I cannot pick 90 days or 1 year**

Those periods belong to the Growth and Pro plans. A crown on the button means your plan does not include it.

**I do not see a Location filter**

It is part of the Pro plan, and it appears only when your business has two or more locations.

**Analytics is not in my sidebar**

Only owners and admins can open it.
